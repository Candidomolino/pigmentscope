A SMALL, TRANSPARENT FIRST VERSION

What the program measures.

1. Define the regions

Draw the sample rectangle inside a phase, channel, membrane, or droplet region. Use the reference rectangle for a comparable background or blank from the same frame. Keep both rectangles away from text, scale bars, glare, and vessel walls.

2. Select a visible color

The program converts RGB pixels to HSV and selects pixels within a circular hue interval, a minimum saturation, and a minimum brightness. The matched area is a geometric color fraction, not extraction efficiency or purity.

3. Compare the signals

Mean RGB uses every nontransparent sample pixel, independently of the color mask. Hue is a saturation-weighted circular mean. The reference contrast is −log10(mean G of sample / mean G of reference), based on processed camera pixels. It is an empirical color index, not spectrophotometric absorbance.

4. Follow a video

Sampling seeks to evenly spaced playback times and reuses the same fixed regions and thresholds. It does not track moving droplets or compensate for camera motion. Native frame rate, accelerated playback, exposure, lighting, and white balance must be checked from the paper.

5. Interpret within the evidence

RGB cannot establish chemical identity, distinguish overlapping pigments, or recover pigment concentration from an uncalibrated published image. Fluorescence pseudocolors and grayscale microscopy cannot be treated as native visible pigment color.

Reproducibility

CSV includes the filename, source, time, regions, thresholds, working resolution, quality flags, and measurements. The JSON export retains the settings and results together. Large images are analyzed at a working resolution up to 2048 pixels on the longest edge; original and working dimensions are recorded.
